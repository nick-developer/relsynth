\section{Reproducible drafting and optional partner links}\label{sec:v72drafting}
% numbered from 1840; the shared counter is restored at the end
\newcounter{savedpropdraft}\setcounter{savedpropdraft}{\value{proposition}}
The weekly benchmark drafts a specification with \texttt{relsynth introspect} for each dataset and synthesises from the draft unedited. On the MIMIC-IV demo the drafts differed from run to run, and so did the failed checks. Three causes were found, each a class of data rather than one dataset: tables without a primary or unique key were sampled with \texttt{ORDER BY random()}; ratio columns were drafted for code columns that depend only weakly on a parent number; and composite keys were drafted that were not minimal. This section states the three remedies (code \texttt{relsynth/source.py}, \texttt{relsynth/discovery.py}, \texttt{relsynth/introspect.py}). Each proposition is tested by a check in \texttt{tests/science/test\_drafting\_checks.py} (C1840--C1842).

\setcounter{proposition}{1839}
\begin{proposition}[Seeded sample of a table without a key]\label{prop:keylesssample}
Let a table without a key hold the multiset of rows $R$, $|R|=N>n$, let $h_s$ be the 64-bit keyed BLAKE2b hash of a row's values under the seed $s$, and give copy $j=0,1,\dots$ of a row content $v$ (copies ranked among the rows with the same hash) the priority $\pi(v,j)=m(h_s(v)\oplus j\varphi)$, with $\varphi$ the odd constant \texttt{0x9E3779B97F4A7C15} and $m$ the SplitMix64 finaliser. The sample is the $n$ rows of smallest priority. (a) The sample is a function of $R$ and $s$ only: it does not depend on the order in which the database returns the rows. (b) If $h_s$ is modelled as a random function and the rows of $R$ are distinct, the sample is a simple random sample of $n$ rows without replacement, up to ties of probability at most $N^2/2^{65}$. (c) For any $R$, every priority is uniform on $\{0,\dots,2^{64}-1\}$ and the priorities of different contents are independent.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) A row's hash depends on its values and $s$; the rank $j$ of a copy among equal hashes is determined by the multiset, and the copies of one content are interchangeable, so the multiset of (row, priority) pairs, and the $n$ smallest, do not depend on the order. (b) With distinct contents the $j$ are all 0 and $m$ is a bijection of $\mathbb Z_{2^{64}}$ (each step, $x\mapsto x\oplus(x\gg k)$ and multiplication by an odd constant, is invertible), so the priorities are $N$ independent uniform draws; the ranks of i.i.d.\ continuous priorities are a uniform permutation, and its first $n$ elements are a simple random sample (order sampling with uniform priorities, Ros\'en 1997; the bottom-$k$ sketch of Cohen and Kaplan 2007). Two of $N$ uniform 64-bit values coincide with probability at most $\binom N2 2^{-64}$. (c) $x\mapsto m(x\oplus c)$ is a bijection for each constant $c$, so it maps a uniform hash to a uniform priority; contents with different hashes are independent under the model.
\end{proof}
\noindent The priorities of the copies of one content are deterministic functions of one hash, so (b) is not claimed for tables with repeated rows. Check C1840 shows (a) on two physical orders of one table, tests (b) by a chi-square test of each row's inclusion count over 400 seeds against $\mathrm{Binomial}(400,n/N)$, and measures the share of 300 copies of one row in samples of a 600-row table (expected $\frac12$). Tables with a key keep the seeded key sample of v0.9; the hash sample reads the table twice (8 bytes a row in memory) and is used up to \texttt{KEY\_SAMPLE\_MAX\_ROWS} rows (5 million), above which \texttt{ORDER BY random()} is kept and reported in the review list.

\setcounter{proposition}{1840}
\begin{proposition}[A drafted ratio needs a simpler factor]\label{prop:ratiogate}
Let a child column $y$ and a parent number $x$ be observed on $n$ rows, $\hat f=\mathrm{round}_k(y/x)$ the candidate factor of Prop.~\ref{prop:ratio}, and $H$ the plug-in entropy of the empirical law on those rows. Introspect drafts $y=x\,f$ only if, besides the tests of Prop.~\ref{prop:ratio}, $H(\hat f)<H(y)$. (a) If $y=\mathrm{round}_d(xf)$ and $(x,f)\mapsto y$ is injective on the rows, then $H(y)=H(x,f)=H(f)+H(x\mid f)$, so the gate holds unless $x$ is constant within every level of $f$. (b) If $(x,y)\mapsto\hat f$ is injective on the rows (a column $y$ with few values and a parent number with many, unrelated), then $H(\hat f)=H(x,y)\ge H(y)$, and the gate rejects the ratio, however many rows the chance test of Prop.~\ref{prop:ratio} declares significant.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For the empirical law, as for any law, a function $g$ satisfies $H(g(Z))\le H(Z)$ with equality when $g$ is injective on the support of $Z$. (a) $y$ is a function of $(x,f)$, injective by assumption, and the chain rule gives $H(x,f)=H(f)+H(x\mid f)$ with $H(x\mid f)=0$ only if $x$ is a function of $f$. (b) $\hat f$ is a function of $(x,y)$, injective by assumption, and $H(x,y)\ge H(y)$.
\end{proof}
\noindent The gate compares description lengths (minimum description length, Rissanen 1978): a factor model is a categorical attribute, and replacing $y$ by a factor that carries at least as much information gains no structure and adds a derived column that every synthetic row must satisfy. The chance test measures statistical significance, which grows with $n$; the gate measures size of effect, which does not. Each test of a drafted structure draws its permutations from its own generator keyed by the test and its columns (\texttt{discovery.\_step\_rng}), so the outcome of one test does not depend on how many tests ran before it. Check C1841 verifies the identities of (a) and (b) on simulated data and drafts a table with a true three-level discount ($y$ kept as a ratio) and a hierarchical code that depends weakly on a parent's dose count (no ratio) on three seeds of 20{,}000 rows; before the gate the code was drafted as a ratio on all three. On the MIMIC-IV demo the gate removed one such ratio (\texttt{emar\_detail.parent\_field\_ordinal}, KS from 0.19--0.31 to 0.001--0.011 over three seeds) and kept the two ratios of AdventureWorks and the three of Contoso.

\setcounter{proposition}{1841}
\begin{proposition}[Drafted composite keys are minimal]\label{prop:minimalkey}
Let $a$ and $b$ be columns of a table and $T_{ab}$ its rows where both are set. Introspect drafts $\{a,b\}$ as a unique key only if neither $a$ nor $b$ is unique on $T_{ab}$, checked on all rows of the table when the sample is not the whole table. (a) If $a$ is unique on $T_{ab}$, every table that satisfies it also satisfies uniqueness of $\{a,b\}$ on $T_{ab}$, so the drafted key adds no constraint that a minimal key does not already give (Codd's candidate key is minimal). (b) A uniqueness constraint is enforced at synthesis by removing rows that break it; a non-minimal key drafted from a sample, where $a$ is unique on $T_{ab}$ by chance or by the source's layout, makes the engine remove synthetic rows whose $b$ repeats within an $a$, though the source has none to compare with.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) Two rows of $T_{ab}$ with equal $(a,b)$ have equal $a$. (b) follows from the enforcement step, which keeps the first row of each key value.
\end{proof}
\noindent A frequent source of such keys is a header-and-detail layout, where one row per parent carries header fields and the others the details: on $T_{ab}$ the parent key is then unique by itself. Check C1842 drafts such a table and a table with a true composite grain on three seeds, and checks every drafted two-column key against the whole table; the grain is kept. On the MIMIC-IV demo eight such keys of \texttt{emar\_detail} were drafted; without them the share of rows removed fell from 0.15 to 0.

\subsection*{Rows without a partner}
A fact row is generated from its anchor row and, for a fact with two dimensions, a partner row (Sections v0.4--v0.7). Before 2.3 the engine left out of the model every row whose anchor or partner key did not resolve, NULL keys included, and reported their number. When the partner link is optional, the rows without a partner are a part of the table, not noise: on the MIMIC-IV demo, 35{,}828 of the 72{,}018 rows of \texttt{emar\_detail} are header rows (one per administration) with no pharmacy and the header fields set, and none of them were synthesised (null-rate differences of 0.4--0.5 on every header field). With \texttt{facts.null\_partner: model} (the default \texttt{auto} is \texttt{model}, except in stage 2 of DP mode, where a NULL key of the seed database is stage-1 noise and Prop.~\ref{prop:post}'s valid links are kept; \texttt{drop} restores 2.2) they are modelled as follows (code \texttt{Synthesizer.\_null\_partner}, \texttt{\_post\_null\_partner}).

\setcounter{proposition}{1842}
\begin{proposition}[A null level of the partner]\label{prop:nullpartner}
Let $N^0_u$ be the number of rows of anchor row $u$ with a NULL partner key, $z_u$ the anchor's segment, and let these counts follow the single-dimension count model of the engine: $N^0_u\mid z_u=k\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda^0_k)$ with $\lambda^0_k\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(a,b)$, or the Dirichlet-process count law of Prop.~\ref{prop:countbf} when it wins by the count-model Bayes factor, independent of the rows with a partner given the segments. Let the attributes of those rows have their own groups $(\varnothing,k)$, pooled toward one coarse group of all such rows, and each extra dimension its own segment law given $k$. Then (a) the joint law of a row's (partner, attributes, extra keys) given the anchor segment $k$ is a mixture with weight $\lambda^0_k/(\lambda^0_k+\Lambda_k)$ on ``no partner'' ($\Lambda_k$ the expected rows with a partner per anchor row of segment $k$), so the share of NULL partners, their number per anchor row and the attributes given a NULL partner are fitted, not set to zero; (b) $N^0$ and the attributes of the rows without a partner are fitted given the anchor segments that the 2.2 model finds from the rows with a partner, so those segments, the blocks of the rows with a partner and their attribute groups are the 2.2 ones (given the same random stream up to that point); (c) with no NULL partner key in the source, or with \texttt{drop}, the model, its random stream and its output are those of 2.2; (d) orphan keys (set, with no parent row) are still left out and counted.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) Rows with and without a partner are drawn independently given the anchor rows and segments, with means $\Lambda_k$ and $\lambda^0_k$ per anchor row; the share of a row of either kind given $k$ is the ratio of the means, and each kind's attributes come from its own groups. (b) $N^0$ is not a relation of segment discovery: the Gibbs sampler targets the 2.2 likelihood, and $\lambda^0$ and the new groups are drawn after it, from their posteriors given $z$. (c) When no row has a NULL partner, \texttt{\_null\_partner\_mask} returns nothing, no relation, group or extra law is created and no random number is drawn. (d) The mask requires a NULL key, so an unresolved non-NULL key keeps the 2.2 treatment.
\end{proof}
\noindent Letting $N^0$ take part in segment discovery is also a valid model, and was tried first: on the MIMIC-IV demo (one header row per anchor row, so $N^0$ carries almost no information) it coarsened the anchor segments, and the attributes of the rows with a partner moved away from the source (product unit TAB 43\% in the source, 31\% synthetic on seed 1, against 39\% under 2.2 and 40\% with (b)). The assumption of (a) is conditional independence of the two kinds of rows given the anchor segment, the same assumption the engine makes between the rows of different links. A fact whose rows are the parent of a nested fact, and a virtual partner, keep the 2.2 treatment, and the exported model draws only rows with a partner (it says so in its scope list). Check C1843 builds a header-and-detail table (one header row per anchor with a status and no partner, one to three detail rows with a partner and a dose, an extra link that differs between the two kinds) on three seeds, in memory and with push-down, and compares the share of NULL partners, the header rows per anchor, the attribute patterns of each kind and the extra link given a NULL partner with the source; with \texttt{drop} no header row is drawn.
