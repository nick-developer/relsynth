\section{Role-playing links and match-aware link affinity}\label{sec:v75rolelinks}
\newcounter{savedproprole}\setcounter{savedproprole}{\value{proposition}}
A fact can link to one parent table several times: a payment and a receiving currency, an origin and a destination airport, a ship-from and a ship-to warehouse (a \emph{role-playing} dimension). Two links of a row can also point at parents that share a value more often than chance (\texttt{link\_affinity}): the same account (a self-loop), the same bank, the same region. Both cases appeared on the IBM AML benchmark (71\,\% cross-currency rows against 1.4\,\% in the data; no reinvestments, which are always self-loops) and were reproduced on two more data classes before the change (synthetic currency transfers and regional shipments, \texttt{tests/unit/test\_joint\_links\_match\_affinity.py}). The code is \texttt{Synthesizer.\_fit\_joint\_extras}, \texttt{\_joint\_extra\_rows}, \texttt{\_match\_flag\_layout} and \texttt{\_match\_links} in \texttt{relsynth/engine.py}.

\setcounter{proposition}{1869}
\begin{proposition}[Role-playing extra links drawn jointly]\label{prop:rolelinks}
Let a fact have extra links $x_1,\dots,x_m$ ($m\ge2$) to one parent table, none set by an offset, with segment labels $s_j$ (level $K_j$ for a null key, when present) and block $b=(z_1,z_2)$. (a) Drawn one by one given the block (the model before this change), $P(s_1=c_1,\dots,s_m=c_m\mid b)=\prod_j P_j(c_j\mid b)$; in particular for a parent with one segment per row, the share of rows whose links agree is $\sum_c\prod_j P_j(c\mid b)$, which the margins fix whatever the source's agreement. (b) The joint model draws the level $S=(s_1,\dots,s_m)$ from $P(S\mid b)$, a sparse Dirichlet over the $L=\prod_j d_j$ joint levels with a back-off prior towards $P(S\mid\text{the more informative block dimension})$, exactly as \texttt{\_extra\_dist} does for one link, and then each link's row within its segment by its degree weight, independently. For a parent with one segment per row the law of the rows given $b$ is $P(S\mid b)$, whose posterior mean converges to the source's conditional frequency of each combination in blocks with growing counts. (c) A combination held by fewer than $k$ source rows ($k=$ \texttt{privacy.suppress\_small\_counts}) is not a level of the joint law: its rows are counted at one extra level, ``other'', and a row drawn there takes each link from its own law given the block reweighted by $r_j(c)=q_j(c)/p_j(c)$, where $p_j$ is link $j$'s margin over all source rows and $q_j=(n^{o}_j+d_jp_j)/(n^{o}+d_j)$ its margin over the ``other'' rows smoothed towards $p_j$. So each link's margin keeps the floored rows' mass where it was, and a rare combination appears only at the rate independent links give it (times the share of the ``other'' level), never from its own count, as a rare level of one column is not reproduced. (d) The joint law is used only when the Gunel--Dickey Bayes factor of the joint table (every row) against independent margins is at least $B=$ \texttt{facts.joint\_links\_bf}, and $L\le$ \texttt{facts.joint\_links\_max\_levels}. The joint prior is a symmetric Dirichlet with total concentration $c=\sum_j d_j$ and the margin priors are its margins, $\mathrm{Dir}(c/d_j)$, so both models have the same prior mean. Under independence $\log\mathrm{BF}\to-\infty$ at rate $\tfrac12\big(L-1-\sum_j(d_j-1)\big)\log n$; under dependence it grows linearly in $n$. (e) A fact without such a group consumes the same random numbers in the same order as before, so its output is byte-identical.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) The single-link draws use separate calls of \texttt{rng.choice} with the block's probabilities and no shared variable, so they are independent given $b$; the agreement share of per-row segments is the sum over rows of the product of the margins. (b) \texttt{\_level\_dist} performs the operations of \texttt{\_extra\_dist} on the joint labels $S=\mathrm{ravel}(s_1,\dots,s_m)$; the level is decoded by \texttt{unravel\_index} with the same dimensions, and the rows of distinct links are drawn by separate weighted choices within their segments. With one segment per row a level is a tuple of rows, so the law of the rows is $P(S\mid b)$. The Dirichlet posterior mean $(n_{b,S}+\alpha_S)/(n_b+\sum\alpha)$ converges to $n_{b,S}/n_b$ as $n_b\to\infty$. (c) Rows of a floored combination carry the label $L$ (``other'') in \texttt{\_level\_dist}, so no joint level has their combination's count. A row drawn at level $L$ takes each link's segment from \texttt{extra\_p}, the single-link law given its block, times $r_j$, independently, so the expected number of rows on a given rare combination is the share of level $L$ times the product of the reweighted single-link probabilities. Averaged over blocks, $\sum_bP(b)P_j(c\mid b)\approx p_j(c)$, so the reweighted law has margin $\approx q_j$ at level $L$, and link $j$'s margin over all rows is $(1-\phi)p^{k}_j+\phi q_j\approx p_j$, with $\phi$ the floored share and $p^k_j$ the margin of the kept rows; without $r_j$ it would be $(1-\phi)p^k_j+\phi p_j$, which moves mass from the levels of rare combinations to common ones. $q_j$ reads per-level counts of one link, as the single-link law already does, and no combination's count. (d) The marginal likelihood of a table under a Dirichlet prior is the Dirichlet-multinomial; aggregating $\mathrm{Dir}(c/L)$ over the other links gives $\mathrm{Dir}(c/d_j)$ on link $j$'s margin, so both models have the same prior mean, and the multinomial coefficient cancels (Good 1976; Gunel and Dickey 1974). With $c=L$ (one per cell) the margin priors concentrate near uniform when $L\gg n$, and skewed independent margins then read as dependence (three Zipf links of 13 levels and 400 rows gave $\log\mathrm{BF}=+91$; the PR bench's stress schema hit this); $c=\sum_j d_j$ gives negative values on such tables at every size tried and keeps strong dependence decisive (\texttt{test\_log\_bf\_joint\_detects\_dependence\_only}). The rates are the usual Laplace (BIC) rates of nested multinomial models: the saturated model has $L-1$ free parameters and the independence model $\sum_j(d_j-1)$. (e) \texttt{\_extra\_dist} calls \texttt{\_level\_dist} with the labels and level count it computed before, which runs the same draws in the same order; \texttt{\_fit\_joint\_extras} registers nothing and \texttt{\_fact\_rows} skips no link when no group passes (d). The CI identity job checks this on every fake scenario and the quickstart.
\end{proof}
\noindent Links whose parent is synthesised with few segments (a warehouse table whose segments do not follow its regions) gain little from (b): the dependence runs through an attribute of the parent, which is what \texttt{link\_affinity} declares (Prop.~\ref{prop:matchrows}).

\setcounter{proposition}{1870}
\begin{proposition}[Link-affinity matches as a row attribute]\label{prop:matchrows}
Let a fact declare a match $(a,b)$ whose moved link $\ell$ (the link of $b$) is the partner or an extra link, with no fixed share, no unique key over $\ell$, no nesting, $\ell$ not the partner of an occupancy fact (whose kept pairs a moved partner would break), and $a$ a path through a link, a lookup column (read through its path) or a column the attribute model draws (a column drawn after the links keeps the share model), and let $M=\mathbf 1[a=b]$ on each source row. $M$ is added to the fact's attribute model as a binary column, joined with the categoricals of every check rule that reads both links (under the floor of the rule-linked joints). At sampling, the attributes and $M$ come from the generator, $\ell$ from the link model $Q(\ell\mid\text{row})$; a row whose link disagrees with $M$ is redrawn as follows. (a) If both paths read through links of a role-playing group (Prop.~\ref{prop:rolelinks}), the group's level is drawn from $P(S\mid b)\,p_M(S)$ with $p_1(S)=\sum_{i\in s_a,\,j\in s_b}w_iw_j\mathbf 1[v_a(i)=v_b(j)]/(W_aW_b)$ and $p_0=1-p_1$, the first row by weight and the second among its segment's rows that agree with $M$. Then the final keys have the law $Q(\cdot\mid M)$ of the joint model. (b) Otherwise the row's link is moved to a parent drawn by degree weight among the parents with (if $M=1$) or without (if $M=0$) the row's value of $a$; the law of the moved link given $M$ is the weight law restricted to the agreeing parents. (c) In both cases the joint law of the attributes and $M$ on the released rows is the generator's, so a rule that ties an attribute to the match on every source row (``a reinvestment is a self-loop'') holds on every synthetic row whose match column the generator draws within the rule's support (always, when the rule-linked joint is formed), without rejecting rows. (d) Entries are applied in the declared order, and a redraw that would break an earlier entry's agreement, or a key or attribute rule that held on the row, is not made, so a later entry never undoes an earlier one and a move never makes a valid row invalid; a moved partner moves the row's block to its segment. (e) $M$ is a function of columns the model already reads; it adds one binary column and no new source value, and under DP mode it is part of the table the DP mechanism covers.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) Write $A$ for agreement with $M$. The kept row has the law $Q(x)\mathbf 1[A(x)]$; a disagreeing row (probability $Q(\neg A)$) is replaced by a draw from $Q(\cdot\mid A)$. So $P(x)=Q(x)\mathbf 1[A(x)]+Q(\neg A)\,Q(x\mid A)=Q(x\mid A)\big(Q(A)+Q(\neg A)\big)=Q(x\mid A)$. The redraw is $Q(\cdot\mid A)$: the level's probability of agreeing is $p_M(S)$ because the rows are drawn independently by weight within their segments, so $P(S\mid b,A)\propto P(S\mid b)p_M(S)$, and given $S$ and the first row, the second row's law restricted to agreeing rows is its weight law conditioned on $A$. (b) The draw is from the weights restricted to the agreeing set by construction (rejection for $M=0$, at most eight rounds; rows still disagreeing keep their link and are left to the rule checks). (c) $M$ and the attributes are drawn together by the generator before the links are adjusted, and the adjustment reads $M$ but changes neither; a categorical joint over observed combinations has the source's support. (d) \texttt{\_keeps\_done} compares each earlier entry's agreement before and after the redraw and keeps only rows that do not lose it. (e) The column is computed from the source's keys and their parents' values, which the link model and the rules already read; it enters \texttt{TableModel} like any categorical column, so every generator and DP mechanism that covers the table covers it.
\end{proof}
\noindent The share model (Goodman's diagonal inflation, \texttt{\_apply\_affinity}) remains for the cases listed and with \texttt{model: share} or \texttt{structure.link\_affinity: share}. On the test fact with reinvestments it keeps the self-loop share (10.3\,\% against 10.4\,\%) but loses the reinvestments (0.2\,\% against 8.0\,\%), because the self-loops are placed after the rule checks; with the match as a row attribute the reinvestments are 7.7\,\% and the other self-loops 2.7\,\% (source 2.6\,\%).
