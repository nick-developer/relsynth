Gap plan G4 closes fidelity gaps found by the time-series, document and autotune work, and gives binary columns (images, documents, files) synthetic content; relsynth 2.1 wrote them as NULL (guide \texttt{docs/binary.md}; code \texttt{relsynth/binary.py}). \subsection*{Binary columns}
A binary column's source values $v_1,\dots,v_N$ (some null) are summarised by the number of nulls $N_0$ and, for each non-null value, a cell $c(v)=(m(v),\ell(v))$: $m(v)$ is the MIME type read from the first bytes (pooled into \texttt{application/octet-stream} when the type has fewer than $k$ values, $k=\texttt{privacy.suppress\_small\_counts}$), and $\ell(v)$ is the length $|v|$ itself when at least $k$ values of type $m(v)$ have that length, otherwise the bin $\lfloor\log_2|v|\rfloor$. With counts $n_c$, a dataset draws $p\sim\mathrm{Beta}(N_0+\tfrac12,\,N-N_0+\tfrac12)$ and $w\sim\mathrm{Dir}(n_c+\tfrac12)$ (or uses their means), and each synthetic value is null with probability $p$, otherwise of cell $c\sim w$, with length $\ell$ or $\lfloor 2^{b+U}\rfloor$, $U\sim\mathrm{U}(0,1)$, for a bin $b$; its content is a fixed placeholder file of type $m$ (size $s_m$) padded with zero bytes to length $\max(\ell,s_m)$, or uniform random bytes of length $\ell$; the length law is therefore exact above $s_m$ and lengths below $s_m$ are raised to it.

\setcounter{proposition}{1779}
\begin{proposition}[The binary stream changes nothing else]\label{prop:binstream}
Let the data stream $G$ of a dataset have state $s$ when a table's binary columns are filled. The values of column $c$ are drawn from a Philox generator keyed by $(\texttt{binary}, t, c, H(s))$, $H$ a hash of the state that does not advance $G$. Then (a) $G$'s sequence of draws, and so every other column and table of the dataset, is the same for every value of \texttt{binary.generator}, and (b) two partitions or datasets whose data streams differ at that point get independent binary values (up to hash collisions of probability $2^{-128}$).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) Reading $G$'s state is not a draw, and the binary values are drawn only from the derived generator, so the draws taken from $G$ before and after are identical; every later column and table reads $G$ only. (b) Different states give different keys except on a collision of the 128-bit key (SHA-256 truncated, \texttt{relsynth.runtime.streams}), and Philox streams of distinct keys are treated as independent (Prop.~950).
\end{proof}
\noindent Check C1780 fits a star schema with a binary photo column, samples it with the generator \texttt{none} and \texttt{placeholder}, and compares every other column byte for byte.

\setcounter{proposition}{1780}
\begin{proposition}[What a binary column releases]\label{prop:binrelease}
Given the cell counts $(N_0, n_c)$, the synthetic values are independent of the source bytes. In particular (a) no synthetic value is a function of the content of a source value beyond its type and length class; (b) a length shared by fewer than $k$ values of its type is released only through its bin's count; (c) for \texttt{random}, a synthetic value of length $L$ equals a given source value with probability at most $256^{-L}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The generator reads the source only through $N_0$ and the counts $n_c$ (the fitted object stores nothing else), and draws content from relsynth's placeholders and the binary stream, which is independent of the data (Prop.~\ref{prop:binstream}). (a) and (b) follow from the definition of the cells. (c) The $L$ bytes are uniform and independent of the source value, so the match probability is $256^{-L}$ for a source value of length $L$ and zero for any other length.
\end{proof}
\noindent The type and length summary is not differentially private (an exact length with $k$ or more values is released), so DP mode keeps binary columns NULL. Check C1781 fits a column of 2{,}000 values with one shared "no image" length, compares the synthetic null share, exact length and bin shares with the source under their posterior intervals, and checks that no synthetic value equals a source value.

\subsection*{Counts of a link under rejection and segment totals}
A count-model link draws the number of rows of each of the $M$ parents of a segment from the DP posterior predictive $F$ (masses $f$ on $0,1,\dots$) conditioned on their sum $R$, by a pairwise Gibbs chain. Two gaps were found on Contoso and AdventureWorks: rows rejected by the exact rule sampler were redrawn as an independent second draw added to the counts already held, and the chain started from the balanced allocation.

\setcounter{proposition}{1781}
\begin{proposition}[Counts given what parents already hold]\label{prop:basecounts}
Let parents hold $b_j\ge 0$ accepted rows and let $R$ new rows be allocated. The pairwise chain whose pair update redraws $x$ from $f(b_i+x)\,f(b_j+s-x)$, $0\le x\le s$, $s=N_i+N_j$, leaves $\pi(N)\propto\prod_j f(b_j+N_j)\,\mathbf 1[\sum_j N_j=R]$ invariant, so the final counts $b_j+N_j$ follow $F$ conditioned on their total, not the law of $b_j$ plus an independent draw. Started from $M$ i.i.d.\ draws from $f$ brought to the total by single-member restricted redraws, every allocation with $\pi>0$ is reachable, and the chain begins with counts in the support of $f$; from the balanced start a large count needs a sequence of pair moves that each have small probability, so within a fixed number of sweeps such counts are rarely reached.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The update is the exact conditional of $\pi$ given the other members and $N_i+N_j=s$, a Gibbs step, so $\pi$ is invariant. Pair moves transfer any amount between two members, so from any start with $\pi>0$ every allocation with $\pi>0$ is reached in at most $M-1$ moves when all intermediate allocations have positive mass; a start whose members are draws from $f$ holds counts in the support of $f$ at the outset. From the balanced start a single pair update produces no count above the pair's current sum ($\le 2\lceil R/M\rceil$ at first), so a count $c$ needs about $\log_2(cM/R)$ successive growing moves, each of small probability under $\pi$: reachable, but rarely within the chain's few sweeps. The restricted redraws keep each member in $[0,R]$ and stop at the total.
\end{proof}
\noindent Check C1782 compares the counts of a segment drawn with and without a rejection round with exact enumeration of the conditional law on a small support.

\setcounter{proposition}{1782}
\begin{proposition}[Segment totals follow the count law]\label{prop:segtotals}
Rows choose a parent segment from $\phi_{z,\cdot}$, so segment $t$'s total $R_t$ is multinomial with variance near its mean, while the sum of $M_t$ counts from $F_t$ has mean $M_t\mu_t$ and variance $M_t\sigma_t^2$. The Metropolis--Hastings kernel with independence proposals $t'\sim\phi_{z_i,\cdot}$ and acceptance $\min\{1,\ \mathcal N(R_{t'}+1;m_{t'},v_{t'})\,\mathcal N(R_s-1;m_s,v_s)/(\mathcal N(R_{t'};m_{t'},v_{t'})\,\mathcal N(R_s;m_s,v_s))\}$, moves that leave a segment under its mandatory total rejected, leaves $\prod_i\phi_{z_i,t_i}\prod_t\mathcal N(R_t;m_t,v_t)\,\mathbf 1[R\ge\mathrm{need}]$ invariant.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a move of row $i$ from $s$ to $t'$ the proposal ratio $q(s)/q(t')=\phi_{z_i,s}/\phi_{z_i,t'}$ cancels the $\phi$ factor of the target, leaving the ratio of the Normal factors of the two totals that change; the indicator is enforced by rejection. This is the standard independence MH acceptance, which satisfies detailed balance.
\end{proof}
\noindent Over totals, the target is the multinomial law of $\phi$ times the Normal factors, so when $\phi$ agrees with the count law in the mean (the fitted case) the precisions add and the totals' spread falls from the multinomial value towards $\sqrt{M_t\sigma_t^2}$. Check C1783 draws totals for segments with under-dispersed count laws and checks their mean and that spread.

\subsection*{Sequence calendars}
\setcounter{proposition}{1783}
\begin{proposition}[One calendar unless the segments differ]\label{prop:poolcal}
Let $n_{kb}$ be the events of segment $k$ in calendar bucket $b$. Under a uniform Dirichlet prior on each bucket profile, the log Bayes factor of separate profiles against one shared profile is $\sum_k[\log B(n_{k\cdot}+\mathbf 1)-\log B(\mathbf 1)]-[\log B(n_{\cdot\cdot}+\mathbf 1)-\log B(\mathbf 1)]$ ($B$ the multivariate Beta function, $n_{\cdot\cdot}$ the pooled counts). Pooling unless it exceeds $\log 100$ (calendar \texttt{auto}) chooses separate calendars with probability tending to one when the profiles differ and to zero when they are equal. With the calendar at its posterior mean (\texttt{calendar\_draw: false}) the variance of a synthetic bucket share is the predictive variance of the events alone; drawing the calendar too adds the posterior variance a second time on top of the per-entity life-time and gap draws.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Dirichlet-multinomial marginal likelihoods give the factor. When profiles are equal the factor is $O_p(1)$ minus a term growing like $\tfrac{(K-1)(B-1)}{2}\log n$ (BIC), so it falls under any fixed threshold; when they differ it grows linearly in $n$. The variance statement is the law of total variance: the synthetic share given the calendar has the events' multinomial variance, and a drawn calendar adds $\mathrm{Var}(\text{calendar}\mid\text{data})$, which the source's realised profile does not carry.
\end{proof}
\noindent Check C1784 fits sequences from one shared seasonal profile and from two different ones, and checks the choice and the bucket shares.

\subsection*{Small set arrays}
A set array of scalars (tags, genres, verifications) is either a child table, whose elements the engine draws one by one given the holder, or one $0/1$ column per element value on the holder (multi-hot), modelled jointly with the holder's attributes by the copula (a multivariate probit). 2.2 chose multi-hot for arrays of at most 16 distinct values, a limit tuned on two databases; the airbnb failure that kept it at 16 was a drafted rule unrelated to the array (\texttt{number\_of\_reviews} $=$ count of reviews, true on 1{,}497 of 1{,}500 listings, accepted by a sample of 400 listings about half of the time; introspection now tests enough parents, Section~\ref{sec:v08}). From 2.3 every set array whose structure allows it is multi-hot, up to a cost bound (code \texttt{relsynth/documents/sets.py}; options \texttt{multihot: auto | on | off} and \texttt{multihot\_max\_columns}): a child table draws its elements one by one with replacement, so its support is not the sets and it needs repairs (analytics' account products, 6 values, drawn as a child table: 1{,}291 \texttt{set} repairs). The model report records how much the joint model carries.
\setcounter{proposition}{1784}
\begin{proposition}[Multi-hot set arrays]\label{prop:multihot}
Let a set array of scalars of one type have $V\ge2$ distinct values over $N$ arrays, with element counts $n_a$ and co-occurrence counts $n_{ab}$, and let $I_{ab}$ be the mutual information of the indicators of $a$ and $b$ from their $2\times2$ table. (i) The recorded $\Delta=2N\sum_{(a,b)\in T}I_{ab}-(V-1)\log N$, for the maximum spanning tree $T$ of the $I_{ab}$, is twice the log-likelihood ratio of the maximum-likelihood tree-structured law over independent indicators, less the BIC penalty of its $V-1$ extra parameters; $\Delta>0$ says the elements' dependence is supported, so that a model of independent elements would lose it. (ii) Shredding writes one cell per value, $1$ if the array holds it and $0$ if not, and the length $n$; nesting takes the held values, matches them to the modelled length $\hat n$ (dropping a uniform random subset, or adding absent values drawn without replacement with the synthetic column shares, seeded by holder) and lists them by the order model. Then (a) the round trip of source documents keeps every set, and every array when the source's arrays never disagree on the order of two values; (b) a synthetic array never repeats a value (no \texttt{set} repair); (c) its length is $\hat n$ clipped to the source's length bounds. (iii) When the source's orders disagree, the order is a Plackett--Luce draw with Bradley--Terry worths $w$ fitted by minorisation--maximisation to the precedence counts (with half a comparison each way per co-observed pair), so $P(a\text{ before }b)=w_a/(w_a+w_b)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) Chow and Liu \cite{chowliu1968}: among tree-structured laws the maximum-likelihood one is given by the maximum spanning tree of the pairwise mutual informations, and its log-likelihood exceeds that of independent indicators by $N\sum_{T}I_{ab}$; each edge adds one parameter to a pair of binary indicators (three cell probabilities instead of two marginals), so $\Delta$ is the difference of the BIC values \cite{schwarz1978} and $\Delta>0$ is the BIC preference, an approximation of a Bayes factor larger than one \cite{kassraftery1995}. (ii)(a) A held value has cell $1$, so the round trip holds the source's set; with no conflicting pair the precedence relation of the source is acyclic and the stored order is a linear extension of it, which lists every source array in its source order. (b) Each value has one cell and added values are drawn among absent ones without replacement. (c) By construction. (iii) A Plackett--Luce draw sorts $\log w_a+G_a$ with i.i.d.\ standard Gumbel $G_a$ (Yellott \cite{yellott1977}); for two values $P(\log w_a+G_a>\log w_b+G_b)=w_a/(w_a+w_b)$, the Bradley--Terry probability whose likelihood the MM iteration of Hunter \cite{hunter2004} maximises (the pseudo-comparisons keep the maximiser finite when one value always precedes another).
\end{proof}
\noindent The cost bound \texttt{multihot\_max\_columns} (default 64) is a bound on work, not on evidence: the copula's work grows with the square of its columns. Larger vocabularies (mflix languages, 234 values) stay child tables; splitting them into frequent values (multi-hot) and the rest (child table) is a follow-up. Check C1785 tests (i) on independent and bundled elements (positive for bundles, not positive for independent elements in at least 90\,\% of samples), (ii)(a)--(c), and (iii) against simulated Plackett--Luce orders.

\subsection*{Inclusion one hop further, and stocks}
\setcounter{proposition}{1785}
\begin{proposition}[Inclusion levels through a parent's key]\label{prop:twohop}
A link from $t$ to parent $p$ with $D$ distinct referenced rows among $n$, and a categorical column $g.c$ of $p$'s parent $g$ reached by $p$'s key: under random selection of the referenced rows, a level held by $n_\ell$ rows of $p$ has no referenced row with probability at most $(1-n_\ell/n)^D\le e^{-Dn_\ell/n}$. Excluding the level when none is referenced and $Dn_\ell/n\ge m$ (\texttt{inclusion\_min\_expected}, default 10) is the rule of the one-hop case applied to the column $g.c$ mapped to $p$'s rows, and a category whose rows are spread over many subcategories is found although no subcategory alone reaches the threshold. With $L$ levels tested for the link (over every candidate column), requiring $Dn_\ell/n\ge m+\log L$ (\texttt{inclusion\_multiplicity: bonferroni}) keeps the probability of excluding any level that the link does reference at random at most $e^{-m}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The mapped column is a function of $p$'s rows, so the hypergeometric bound of the one-hop rule applies unchanged; the number of rows at a category is the sum over its subcategories, which can reach the threshold when none of the terms does. For the multiplicity statement, a level is wrongly excluded only if it has no referenced row, of probability at most $e^{-Dn_\ell/n}\le e^{-m}/L$; by the union bound over the $L$ levels (Bonferroni, Dunn \cite{dunn1961}) the probability of any wrong exclusion is at most $e^{-m}$. The more candidates (the parent's own keys, the columns one hop further) are tested, the more evidence each exclusion needs; \texttt{inclusion\_max\_levels} (default 50) only says which columns are tested level by level.
\end{proof}

\setcounter{proposition}{1786}
\begin{proposition}[Floor of a running balance]\label{prop:stockfloor}
With a floor $0$, the running balance whose outflow on a row is cut to what the balance holds is never negative and equals the plain running sum on every row where the plain sum stays non-negative. Without a floor, i.i.d.\ steps of mean $0$ and variance $\sigma^2>0$ give a random walk whose share of negative rows over a horizon $n\gg(y_0/\sigma)^2$ has a non-degenerate limit law (arcsine), so a positive share of rows is negative; with positive drift the share tends to $0$, with negative drift to $1$. Let $p$ be the source's share of negative rows and $q$ that of the plain running sum of the synthetic steps. Under \texttt{rules.balance\_floor: auto} the floor is applied exactly when $q>2p$; this choice minimises $|\text{synthetic share}-p|$ over the two options (floor: share $0$; no floor: share $q$), and is deterministic given the drawn steps.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first statement is the definition of the capped update, by induction over the rows of a group. For the second, by Donsker's theorem the walk $y_0+\sum_{i\le t}\varepsilon_i$ over $t\asymp (y_0/\sigma)^2$ rows is close to a Brownian motion started at a fixed multiple of its scale, which spends a positive share of time below 0. For the choice, the floor's error is $p$ and the plain sum's is $|q-p|$; $p<|q-p|$ iff $q>2p$ (for $q\ge0$, $p\ge0$). Both $q$ and the capped balance are functions of the drawn steps and the opening values; no random number is drawn.
\end{proof}
\noindent 2.2 drafted the floor when the source was negative on at most 1\,\% of rows, a cut-off from one database. The rule here needs no cut-off: on AdventureWorks inventory ($p=0.4\,\%$) the plain walk is negative on 11.7\,\% of rows, so the floor is applied and the share falls to 0, the closer of the two to the source; a ledger that is negative on a third of its rows keeps its plain walk unless that walk is negative on two thirds. The source's share is computed from the rows read (the sample under pushdown) and logged with the choice (\texttt{sample\_log.balance\_floor}). A balance never negative in the source always gets the floor, as before. Checks C1786 and C1787 test both propositions on constructed data.
