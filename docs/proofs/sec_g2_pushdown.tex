\section{DP stage 1 inside the database (v2.3)}\label{sec:g2push}
% numbered from 1760 (gap plan G2 range); the shared counter is restored at the end
\newcounter{savedproppush}\setcounter{savedproppush}{\value{proposition}}
Until this release a DP run loaded every owned table into memory before stage~1 of Section~\ref{sec:v25dp}. With \texttt{synthesis.privacy.dp.pushdown: auto} (the default) or \texttt{on}, stage~1 runs inside the source database instead (gap plan G2; guide \texttt{docs/dp.md}, sec.~9; code \texttt{relsynth/dp/rows.py}). The database bounds the contributions with a window function, and every statistic is computed from frequency tables that the database returns: \texttt{SELECT} $a_1,\dots,a_k$\texttt{, COUNT(*)} \ldots\ \texttt{GROUP BY} $a_1,\dots,a_k$, over the bounded rows, read in chunks. The noise, the accountant and the decoding are unchanged. The propositions below show that the released statistics are the same functions of the bounded rows as in memory, so the sensitivities, the charges and the guarantee of Props.~\ref{prop:sens}--\ref{prop:run} and \ref{prop:pairsens}--\ref{prop:selrun} carry over. Each is tested by a check in \texttt{tests/science/test\_dp\_pushdown\_checks.py} (C1760--C1764); \texttt{tests/integration/test\_servers.py} repeats C1761 and C1760 on PostgreSQL, SQL Server, Oracle, MySQL, MariaDB and the warehouse stand-ins.

\setcounter{proposition}{1759}
\begin{proposition}[Contribution bounding in SQL is stable]\label{prop:sqlbound}
For a child table $t$ with parent $p$ on its chain, let the database keep the rows of $t$ for which (i) a kept row of $p$ has the row's key, and (ii) \texttt{ROW\_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY} the child key \texttt{ORDER BY} $h_s(\kappa(r)),\kappa(r)$\texttt{)} $\le D_t$, where $\kappa(r)$ is the row's primary key (else a unique key, else all its columns) and $h_s$ the dialect's seeded hash with a seed $s$ drawn per run. Then Prop.~\ref{prop:bound} holds for this truncation with the same $c_t$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:bound} uses two facts: a row of $t$ belongs to the unit of its parent row, and the kept rows for one parent row depend only on the rows of $t$ with that parent and on the key. Condition (i) is the first fact applied to the kept rows of $p$. In (ii) the partition is one parent key, and the order key $(h_s(\kappa(r)),\kappa(r))$ is a function of the row and $s$ alone, so the rank of a row within its partition depends only on the rows of that partition. Ties occur only between rows with equal $\kappa$, that is equal rows when $\kappa$ is all the columns; whichever of them the database keeps, the multiset of kept rows is the same. So removing unit $u$ removes the rows of $u$'s partitions and leaves every other partition and its kept rows unchanged, and $u$ keeps at most $D_t$ rows per kept parent row. The induction along the chains is that of Prop.~\ref{prop:bound}. Neither proof needs $s$ to be secret: privacy rests on stability; the secret seed only makes the choice among a parent's rows look random.
\end{proof}
\noindent The truncated rows differ from those of the in-memory path (another hash), but both are valid bounded databases of the same kind, and the statistics below are computed from the database's own. Check C1760 removes the busiest customer of a star schema from the SQLite file: the kept sales of every other customer are unchanged and no customer keeps more than $D$.

\setcounter{proposition}{1760}
\begin{proposition}[Histograms from frequency tables]\label{prop:freq}
Let $B$ be the bounded rows of $t$, and $f(r)\in\mathbb{Z}^d$ depend only on the values $a(r)=(a_1(r),\dots,a_k(r))$ of row $r$. For the frequency table $F=\{(v,n_v)\}$ with $n_v=|\{r\in B: a(r)=v\}|$, $\sum_{r\in B}f(r)=\sum_{(v,n_v)\in F}n_v\,\phi(v)$, where $\phi(a(r))=f(r)$. Every one-way histogram, level count, null count, row count, histogram of rows per parent row and histogram of group sizes released by stage~1 is of this form, with the cells fixed before the read; hence each equals its in-memory value on $B$ and has the sensitivity of Props.~\ref{prop:sens}(a), \ref{prop:levels}, \ref{prop:degree} and \ref{prop:groups}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Group the sum by the value of $a(r)$: the rows with $a(r)=v$ contribute $n_v\phi(v)$. A one-way histogram has $a$ the column and $\phi$ the indicator of the value's cell (or of no cell for a null in a block without a null cell); a level count is the histogram over the levels present; the row count has $k=0$. Rows per parent row: $a(r)$ is the number of rows of the whole child table with the key of $r$, which the database computes with a \texttt{GROUP BY} of the child joined to $B$; it is a function of $r$ and of the child table, as in memory. Group sizes: group $B$ by (parent key, $g$) first; each group is then one ``row'' with $a$ its size, and Prop.~\ref{prop:groups} counts groups. The values are cast with the spec's types after the read, exactly as \texttt{load\_tables} casts them, so $\phi$ is the in-memory map. Equal statistics of equal bounded rows have equal sensitivity.
\end{proof}

\setcounter{proposition}{1761}
\begin{proposition}[Row-level sums from joint frequency tables]\label{prop:joint}
Let $a(r)$ be the tuple of a row's modelled columns, its capped child counts $\min(\deg,D)$ and its parent row's modelled columns (by a join of $B$ with the kept rows of $p$). The second-moment sum of Prop.~\ref{prop:sens}(b) and every candidate two-way histogram of Prop.~\ref{prop:pairsens} are of the form of Prop.~\ref{prop:freq} in $a$. With integer weights $n_v$ the sums are the same integers as in memory.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In Prop.~\ref{prop:sens}(b) a row's term $v(r)$ is the rounded triangle of $S\,\tilde z\tilde z^\top$ with $\tilde z$ the clipped normal scores of $r$; the scores depend on the row's values, its capped degrees and its parent row's values, through released marginals only. So $v(r)=\phi(a(r))$, the clipping and rounding included, and $\sum_r v(r)=\sum_v n_v\phi(v)$ in exact integer arithmetic. A two-way histogram counts rows by the pair of coarse cells of two nodes, and a node's cell is a function of the same values (Section~\ref{sec:v26dpsel}). The selection scores of MST and AIM are functions of these exact histograms and of released quantities, so they are the same too.
\end{proof}

\setcounter{proposition}{1762}
\begin{proposition}[Chunks and the guarantee]\label{prop:chunks}
(a) Reading a frequency table in chunks and adding the partial sums gives the statistics of Props.~\ref{prop:freq} and \ref{prop:joint}. (b) A DP run with stage~1 in the database satisfies the guarantee of Prop.~\ref{prop:run} (and Prop.~\ref{prop:multiunit} with several units), with the same $\rho$ and $\delta$ as the run in memory.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) The partial sums are over disjoint sets of tuples, which together are $F$; integer addition is associative and commutative, and a tuple split across chunks does not occur because each $(v,n_v)$ is one row of the result. (b) By Props.~\ref{prop:sqlbound}--\ref{prop:joint} each mechanism receives a statistic with the sensitivity it is charged for in memory, on a bounded database that satisfies Prop.~\ref{prop:bound}; the choice of each later statistic depends on the earlier noisy outputs only, as in memory; and the accountant, the mechanisms and the post-processing are the same code. The proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:run} therefore applies unchanged. Nothing the database computes leaves it except the frequency tables, which the process reads and does not release.
\end{proof}
\noindent What leaves the database is the frequency tables of the bounded rows, the counts of rows per key and the public tables, never a whole owned table. Their size is the number of distinct tuples: small for categorical columns and degrees, up to the number of rows for the joint tuples of a table with continuous columns, but read in chunks of 200,000, so the memory of stage~1 does not grow with the size of the tables. Check C1761 compares every exact statistic, in the database and in memory, on a two-level chain (customer, invoice, invoice line) with nothing truncated, for \texttt{copula}, \texttt{mst} and \texttt{aim}, in SQLite and in Parquet files read through DuckDB; it finds them identical. Check C1762 repeats it with chunks of 7 tuples. Check C1763 is check C902 on the pushdown path: it removes the busiest customer and eight others from the SQLite file and replays the full run's noisy outputs; every exact statistic moves by at most its charged sensitivity. Check C1764 runs DP end to end with stage~1 in the database: it reads nothing into memory, spends what the in-memory run spends and keeps the links valid.
